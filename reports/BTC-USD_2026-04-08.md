## 1. Overview and Network Model

Bitcoin is the world's first and largest decentralized cryptocurrency by market capitalization. Unlike equities, BTC has no earnings, cash flows, or balance sheet in the traditional sense. Its fundamental value derives from network effects, supply scarcity (hard-capped at 21 million coins), proof-of-work security, and growing adoption as a store of value and settlement layer.

The April 2024 halving reduced the block reward from 6.25 BTC to 3.125 BTC, cutting new annual issuance to approximately 164,250 BTC (~$11.8B at current prices). This represents an annual inflation rate of approximately 0.83%, making Bitcoin scarcer than gold on a stock-to-flow basis.

## 2. Key On-Chain Metrics

### 2.1 Active Addresses

Active addresses have dropped to approximately **544,000 daily** (CryptoQuant data), an 8-year low as of early April 2026. Historically, between 700,000 and 1,000,000 addresses were active daily. This decline is significant and suggests reduced retail engagement, though it may partly reflect the migration of activity to Layer 2 solutions (Lightning Network) and custodial platforms (ETFs) that batch transactions.

### 2.2 Network Transaction Volume

Daily on-chain transactions have recovered to approximately **615,000** as of early April 2026, a 16-month high (CryptoRank, April 7, 2026). This divergence -- rising transaction count but falling active addresses -- suggests consolidation toward larger, institutional-size transactions rather than broad retail usage.

### 2.3 Supply in Profit vs. Loss

According to Glassnode data (February 2026):
- **11.1 million BTC** in profit (coins acquired below current price)
- **8.9 million BTC** in loss (coins acquired above current price)

At the current price of ~$72,000, approximately **55% of supply** is in profit. Historical cycle bottoms occur when profit/loss supply converges. A convergence scenario would imply a bottom near $60,000 based on current cost basis distributions. The current gap is narrowing but has not yet converged, suggesting the market is in a late-stage correction rather than a capitulation bottom.

## 3. Hash Rate and Mining Economics

### 3.1 Hash Rate

The network hashrate has declined significantly:
- **Q1 2026:** 1,066 EH/s (30-day average)
- **Q2 2026 (current):** 1,004 EH/s (-5.8% QoQ)
- **Estimated offline capacity:** 252 EH/s
- **Peak (2025):** ~1,200 EH/s (above 1 ZH/s)

This marks the first quarterly hashrate decline in 6 years. CoinShares forecasts a recovery to ~1.8 ZH/s by end of 2026, conditional on BTC recovering toward higher price levels. The decline is driven by:
- Price collapse from $126,000 (Oct 2025) to ~$65,000 (Feb 2026)
- Oil prices above $100 due to Middle East geopolitical tensions (Strait of Hormuz disruption)
- Miners pivoting capital toward AI/HPC infrastructure

### 3.2 Mining Difficulty

- **Current difficulty:** ~138.97 T (CoinWarz)
- **Recent adjustment:** -7.76% on March 21, 2026 (to 133.79 T)
- **Feb 7, 2026 adjustment:** -11.16% (largest since China mining ban, July 2021)
- **All-time high:** ~155 T (November 2025)

The difficulty has dropped nearly 10% from its all-time high, reflecting the withdrawal of unprofitable mining capacity.

### 3.3 Mining Profitability

- **Average production cost:** ~$88,000 per BTC (Checkonchain difficulty regression model, March 13, 2026)
- **Current price:** ~$71,941
- **Loss per BTC mined:** ~$16,000 (approximately -22% margin)
- **Hashprice:** ~$27.89-$33.30 per PH/s/day (near all-time low of $28, hit Feb 23, 2026)
- Equipment above 25 J/TH efficiency is operating at negative margins

Miners are forced sellers, adding supply pressure to the spot market. The network's self-correcting difficulty mechanism will eventually restore profitability as inefficient miners exit, but the transition period creates sustained selling pressure.

## 4. Supply Analysis

### 4.1 Halving Effect

The April 2024 halving reduced new issuance to 3.125 BTC per block (~450 BTC/day). This is the fourth halving, and the post-halving year (2025) was notably the first in Bitcoin's history to end in the red, breaking the historical pattern of bull runs 12-18 months after halving.

### 4.2 Exchange Reserves

- **Current exchange balance:** ~2.3-2.7 million BTC (~11.5% of circulating supply)
- **Trend:** Declining -- exchange reserves hit all-time lows in late March 2026 (MEXC, March 31, 2026)
- **MacroMicro data (April 7, 2026):** 2,710,382 BTC on exchanges

The declining exchange supply is structurally bullish, indicating coins are being moved to cold storage or long-term custody. However, the rate of decline has slowed as miners increase selling to cover operating losses.

### 4.3 Long-Term Holders (LTH)

- Approximately **65% of Bitcoin supply** has not moved in over 1 year (Bitget, March 2026)
- Long-term holder supply turned from net distribution (-674,000 BTC in Nov 2025) back to accumulation as of April 2026 (CryptoQuant, April 7, 2026)
- This reversal from distribution to accumulation by long-term holders is historically a precursor to price recovery

### 4.4 Institutional & Corporate Holdings

- **MicroStrategy:** ~528,000 BTC (largest corporate holder)
- Corporate bitcoin holdings reached a **record high** in early 2026
- Institutions buying at **2.8x the new mining supply** (BitcoinMagazine)
- 73% of institutional investors plan to increase crypto holdings in 2026 (Developcoins survey, March 2026)
- March 2026: 81,200 BTC institutional inflow -- the most substantial monthly institutional inflow since October 2025

## 6. Valuation Models

### 6.1 Stock-to-Flow (S2F) Model

- **Current S2F ratio:** ~120 (post-2024 halving)
- **PlanB S2F model prediction:** Average $500,000 this cycle
- **Current price:** $71,941 (significantly below model)
- **Assessment:** The S2F model has significantly overestimated price in this cycle. Bitcoin has failed to breach the 3.2 MVRV level for the first time in any cycle, suggesting diminishing cycle amplitude. The model's predictive power appears to be weakening as Bitcoin matures and market structure evolves.

### 6.2 NVT Ratio (Network Value to Transactions)

- **Formula:** Market Cap / Daily On-Chain Transaction Volume
- **Current reading:** The NVT signal entered the lower valuation zone in late 2025 (MEXC, Dec 27, 2025), suggesting undervaluation
- **Interpretation:** A low NVT ratio means the network is processing high transaction value relative to its market cap -- historically bullish. With daily transactions at a 16-month high (615,000), the NVT ratio is supportive of current price levels.

### 6.3 MVRV Ratio (Market Value to Realized Value)

- **Current MVRV:** Estimated between 1.0 and 1.3 (price is above aggregate cost basis but well below overheated levels)
- **Key MVRV levels:**
  - 0.8 band: ~$43,000 (deep value / historical bottom zone)
  - 1.0 band: ~$54,000 (breakeven for average holder)
  - 2.4 band: ~$130,000 (profit-taking zone)
  - 3.2 band: ~$174,000 (extreme overvaluation)
- **Assessment:** At ~1.0-1.3, the MVRV is in the neutral-to-slightly-positive zone. Historically, Bitcoin bottoms between 0.8 and 1.0 MVRV. The current reading suggests BTC is not deeply undervalued but is far from overheated territory.

### 6.4 Realized Price and Thermocap

- **Realized price (approximate):** ~$54,000-$60,000 (implied by the 1.0 MVRV band)
- The current price of $71,941 sits approximately 20-33% above the realized price, indicating the average holder retains a modest profit margin

## 8. Risk Factors

1. **Miner capitulation:** With production costs at $88,000 vs. price at $72,000, forced selling from miners creates sustained supply pressure
2. **Geopolitical headwinds:** Middle East tensions, oil above $100, Strait of Hormuz closure -- all directly impact mining costs
3. **Broken cycle pattern:** The traditional 4-year halving cycle appears disrupted; the post-halving year ended negative for the first time
4. **Active address decline:** 8-year low in active addresses raises questions about organic network growth
5. **ETF flow sensitivity:** Q1 2026 saw $500M in net outflows; institutional flows can amplify both directions
6. **Regulatory uncertainty:** While improving, global regulatory frameworks remain fragmented
