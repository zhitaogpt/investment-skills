## 2. Monetary Policy & Financing Environment

**PBOC stance: Moderately Loose -- the most accommodative in 15+ years.**

PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng confirmed at the NPC session (March 6, 2026) that the central bank will "flexibly and efficiently use RRR cuts, interest rate reductions, and other monetary policy tools" throughout 2026. The PBOC signaled that both internal conditions (stabilizing bank net interest margins) and external conditions (USD rate-cut cycle, stable RMB) support further easing. CITIC Securities projects a 50bp RRR cut in Q1-Q2 2026, releasing approximately RMB 1 trillion in long-term liquidity. [Source: GoogleSearch "PBOC 货币政策 降准 降息 2026年4月"]

**Transmission to Xiechuang Data -- CRITICAL given 81.5% debt-to-asset ratio:**

- **Financing cost sensitivity:** Xiechuang's asset-liability ratio of 81.5% is exceptionally high, even for a capital-intensive business. The company has applied for RMB 100B+ in credit lines for 2026 (compared to 50B+ in 2025 and actual procurement of ~RMB 21.2B in servers). Each 25bp reduction in lending rates on a RMB 50B outstanding debt base would save approximately RMB 125M annually -- material against RMB 11.6B net profit. [Source: GoogleSearch "协创数据 300857 AI服务器 算力 2026"]
- **Liquidity lifeline:** The company's cloud computing/AI rental model requires massive upfront server procurement financed largely by bank credit. A tightening monetary environment would be existential for this business model. The current easing cycle is therefore not merely beneficial -- it is a **necessary condition** for the company's growth strategy.
- **Sci-Tech relending facility:** The PBOC expanded the Sci-Tech relending facility from RMB 800B to 1.2T in January 2026. AI computing infrastructure companies like Xiechuang are prime candidates for preferential rates through this channel.
- **Risk factor:** If easing disappoints or PBOC tightens unexpectedly, the company's high leverage becomes a significant vulnerability. With only RMB 14.55B in cash supporting RMB 21.2B+ in annual server procurement, the margin of safety is thin.

**Assessment: Strongly Favorable, but leverage amplifies both upside and downside.**

## 3. Industrial Policy Analysis

### 3.1 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030) -- AI Computing as National Priority

The 15th Five-Year Plan ("十五五") explicitly prioritizes "strengthening the efficient supply of AI computing power, algorithms, and data" and calls for "building a national integrated computing network with scalable, intensive, green, and inclusive development." The plan mentions AI infrastructure 8 times -- a historic first for such prominence. The "East-to-West Computing" project has established 8 hub nodes and 10 data center clusters nationwide. [Source: GoogleSearch "十五五规划 人工智能 算力 基础设施"]

### 3.2 State Council and Top-Level Policy Signals

The State Council (国常会) issued a directive to "vigorously promote large-scale commercialization of AI applications" and deepen the "AI+" initiative across industries. The 2026 Government Work Report promotes "new-generation intelligent terminals and AI agents," with specific targets for commercialized AI deployment across manufacturing, healthcare, and finance. In January 2026, MIIT and 7 other ministries jointly issued the "AI+Manufacturing" action plan targeting 1,000 high-level industrial AI agents by 2027. [Source: GoogleSearch "国常会 政治局会议 AI 数字经济 2026"]

### 3.3 Shenzhen AI Server Industry Chain Policy (March 2026)

Critically for Xiechuang (headquartered in Shenzhen), the Shenzhen Municipal Government issued the "Action Plan for Accelerating High-Quality Development of the AI Server Industry Chain (2026-2028)" on March 23, 2026. This plan specifically targets AI server supply chain development with:
- **Computing vouchers (算力券/训力券):** Up to RMB 10M per entity, covering 50% of AI computing service costs
- **Equipment renewal subsidies:** Support for industrial equipment upgrades and technology transformation
- **National-level AI computing hub construction:** Shenzhen positioned as a core node
- **Support for 14nm and below advanced chip integration, including domestic GPU/NPU promotion**
[Source: GoogleSearch "深圳 算力基础设施 AI服务器 补贴 政策 2026"]

A national-level "AI Computing Industry Chain Upgrade Plan" was jointly issued by MIIT and NDRC in late March 2026, signaling unprecedented central government commitment. [Source: GoogleSearch "深圳 算力基础设施 AI服务器 补贴 政策 2026"]

**Transmission to Xiechuang Data:**

- **Direct beneficiary of Shenzhen policy:** As a Shenzhen-headquartered AI server and computing services company, Xiechuang is a primary target of the city's 2026-2028 action plan. The company's cloud computing services (算力租赁) business may directly access computing voucher subsidies.
- **Order pipeline validation:** The company's cumulative RMB 32.2B+ in server procurement commitments (RMB 21.2B in 2025, RMB 11B additional in Feb 2026) is directionally aligned with national policy. Over 40% of 2025 revenue came from computing-related services, a share expected to grow.
- **"Thousand-Billion AI Fund":** National-level AI industry funds totaling RMB 100B+ have been established, with specific allocations for computing infrastructure -- directly supporting companies like Xiechuang.

**Assessment: Strongly Favorable. Policy alignment is near-perfect.**

## 4. US-China Technology Rivalry & Export Controls

**Current situation: Elevated and evolving risk -- directly relevant to Xiechuang's GPU supply chain.**

The US export control landscape remains dynamic and hostile. Key developments in 2026:

1. **January 2026:** US BIS revised advanced computing chip licensing policy, allowing exports of NVIDIA H200 and AMD chips to China under specific security requirements -- a partial relaxation. [Source: GoogleSearch "中美贸易 科技制裁 芯片 2026"]
2. **March 2026:** US Commerce Department withdrew a proposed rule ("AI Action Plan Implementation") that would have expanded AI chip export controls globally. This is seen as a temporary reprieve, not a permanent relaxation. [Source: GoogleSearch "AI服务器 出口管制 制裁 2026"]
3. **March 2026:** Trump administration launched the "American AI Exports Program," a new framework for US-led consortia to distribute AI computing resources -- potentially creating an alternative control mechanism. [Source: GoogleSearch "AI服务器 出口管制 制裁 2026"]
4. **April 2026:** US Congressional committees are actively pushing new legislation (including the "Semiconductor Technology Resilience" act) to further restrict semiconductor equipment exports and tighten enforcement against transshipment. [Source: GoogleSearch "AI服务器 出口管制 制裁 2026"]
5. **December 2025:** US announced tariffs on Chinese semiconductor imports effective June 2027, with rates TBD.

**Transmission to Xiechuang Data -- HIGH RELEVANCE:**

- **GPU procurement risk:** Xiechuang's core business model depends on procuring NVIDIA and other high-end GPUs/AI accelerators for server assembly and computing rental services. Any tightening of US export controls on advanced AI chips to China would directly constrain the company's ability to source inventory. The H200/B200-class chips are the lifeblood of Xiechuang's cloud computing business.
- **Domestic substitution pathway:** The Shenzhen policy explicitly promotes "domestic GPU/NPU" integration. Xiechuang has begun diversifying toward Huawei Ascend and other domestic alternatives, but performance parity with NVIDIA's latest generation remains years away. This transition introduces execution risk.
- **Cloud service restructuring:** The US Congress passed the "Remote Access Security Act" in early 2026, potentially bringing cloud computing services under export control jurisdiction. If implemented, this could affect Xiechuang's ability to serve certain international clients or access US-origin cloud infrastructure.
- **Tariff overhang:** The announced (but delayed to mid-2027) semiconductor tariffs create uncertainty for the entire AI hardware supply chain, including server components.

**Assessment: Significant Risk. This is the single largest macro vulnerability for Xiechuang.**

## Conclusion

The macro environment for Xiechuang Data (300857.SZ) presents a tale of two forces. On the positive side, the convergence of China's most accommodative monetary policy in 15+ years, the 15th Five-Year Plan's unprecedented elevation of AI computing infrastructure as a national priority, Shenzhen's dedicated AI server industry chain policy (2026-2028), and a booming domestic AI capex cycle collectively create a **once-in-a-decade policy tailwind** for the company's core business.

On the risk side, two factors demand vigilance. First, the company's 81.5% debt-to-asset ratio and RMB 100B+ credit appetite make it extraordinarily sensitive to monetary policy shifts -- the current easing cycle is not just helpful, it is **structurally necessary** for the business model to function. Second, the US-China technology decoupling -- specifically export controls on advanced AI chips (NVIDIA H200/B200 class) -- represents a potential supply-side chokepoint that could fundamentally constrain Xiechuang's growth trajectory. The March 2026 withdrawal of the AI chip export rule provides temporary relief, but Congressional momentum toward stricter controls remains strong.

Net assessment: the macro backdrop is favorable and supportive of the company's aggressive expansion strategy, but the margin of safety is thinner than for less-leveraged, less import-dependent peers in the AI ecosystem.

**Macro Rating: 7.5/10 -- Favorable with material leverage and geopolitical tail risks.**
