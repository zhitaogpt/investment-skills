## 1. Major Company News Events (Past 1-3 Months)

### 1.1 FY2025 Annual Results -- Record Revenue but Guidance Shock (March 25, 2026)

Pop Mart delivered a blockbuster FY2025 with revenue of **RMB 37.12 billion (+184.7% YoY)** and adjusted net profit of **RMB 13.08 billion (+284.5% YoY)**. Gross margin expanded from 66.8% to 72.1%, reaching a record high. [Source: HKEX Annual Report, Reuters]

**Key financial highlights:**
- THE MONSTERS (Labubu family) revenue broke RMB 10B for the first time, reaching **RMB 14.16B** (~38% of total)
- SKULLPANDA: RMB 3.54B; CRYBABY: RMB 2.93B; MOLLY: RMB 2.90B; DIMOO: RMB 2.78B; Twinkle Twinkle: RMB 2.06B; HIRONO: RMB 1.74B
- **17 artist IPs** generated over RMB 100M in revenue each
- Plush category revenue: **RMB 18.71B (+560.6% YoY)**, becoming the largest product category
- Labubu unit sales surpassed **100 million units** in 2025

**Overseas business was the star:**
- Overseas revenue: **RMB 16.27B (+291.9% YoY)**, accounting for 43.8% of total (up from 31.8%)
- Americas: **RMB 6.81B (+748.4% YoY)**, ranked #1 on TikTok across all categories in the US
- Global store network expanded to **571 stores across 18 countries**

However, the stock crashed 22.5% on the day because:
1. **2026 guidance of "no less than 20%" growth** -- dramatically lower than the 184.7% achieved in 2025, implying sharp deceleration
2. Q4 2025 growth had already slowed significantly vs. Q3's 245-250% pace
3. H2 2025 overseas revenue growth decelerated to ~281%, with Black Friday in North America underperforming expectations [Source: Huxiu, Wall Street CN]

CEO Wang Ning used an F1 metaphor: "It's not about always hitting the accelerator -- when you need a pit stop, you must take one." [Source: Reuters, CNBC]

### 1.2 Sony Pictures / Labubu Movie Announcement (March 18, 2026)

Pop Mart and Sony Pictures officially announced a **live-action/CGI Labubu feature film** in early development. **Paul King** (director of Paddington and Wonka) will direct, with a screenplay co-written by King and **Steven Levenson**. [Source: Forbes, BBC, NYT, Hollywood Reporter]

This is a significant strategic move to transform Labubu from a toy franchise into an entertainment IP, following the Disney/Pixar playbook. The announcement was widely covered across major global media outlets, underscoring Pop Mart's growing international relevance.

**TOP TOY filed its second Hong Kong IPO application** on March 31, 2026, positioning itself as the "#2 designer toy platform." While its revenue grew 94.8% to RMB 1.92B in 2025, profitability declined, and its market share at ~4.8% remains far behind Pop Mart. Its model relies on aggregating third-party IPs via retail stores rather than building proprietary IPs. [Source: 36Kr, Zhihu, Sina Finance]

### 2.2 Labubu Dependency Risk -- The Core Market Concern

The single biggest concern in the market is Pop Mart's heavy reliance on Labubu/THE MONSTERS:
- THE MONSTERS contributed **~40% of total revenue** in 2025, up from 23% in 2024
- Former core IPs (Molly, Crybaby) saw sales growth stagnate relatively
- Secondary market prices for Labubu older series declined in March-April 2026
- New series show narrower secondary premiums vs. previous launches

This is the primary driver of the post-earnings selloff. The market is questioning whether Pop Mart can replicate the Labubu phenomenon with other IPs. [Source: SCMP, Bloomberg, Wall Street CN]

### 2.3 Regulatory Environment

- China and Singapore have introduced or planned **tighter regulations on blind box sales**, reflecting concern about addictive/gambling-adjacent mechanics. Pop Mart has publicly embraced these regulations. [Source: CNBC]

### 2.4 US-China Trade Tensions / Tariff Risk

- Pop Mart has diversified manufacturing to Vietnam, Southeast Asia, Mexico, Cambodia, and Indonesia to mitigate tariff impact [Source: Reuters]
- Despite tariff headwinds, Pop Mart was described as "tariff-proof" due to strong brand loyalty, though Goldman data shows **US sales fell 46% YoY in March 2026** -- potentially tariff-related or demand normalization [Source: Goldman Sachs via Wall Street CN]
- Management has shifted focus to "supply chain efficiency over tariff strategy" [Source: CNBC]
