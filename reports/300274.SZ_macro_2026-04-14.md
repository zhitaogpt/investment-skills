## 3. Industry-Specific Policy and Regulation Changes

### 3.1 China: Solar Export Tax Rebate Cancellation (Effective April 1, 2026)

This is **the single most important near-term policy change** for Sungrow.

- **Policy:** Ministry of Finance/State Administration of Taxation announced (January 8, 2026) that effective April 1, 2026, the 9% VAT export tax rebate for all solar products — including **inverters** — is fully eliminated. Battery products face a phased reduction (9% to 6% through Dec 2026, then zero from Jan 2027).
- **Scope:** Covers 249 product categories across the entire solar value chain: polysilicon, wafers, cells, modules, inverters.
- **Industry Impact:** Export costs increase by ~9% for inverters immediately. This accelerates the shakeout of smaller, low-margin producers and benefits scale leaders like Sungrow who can absorb or pass through the cost.
- **Sungrow-Specific Impact:**
  - **Short-term headwind:** ~9 percentage points of export cost increase on inverter exports; impacts are partially mitigated by Sungrow's overseas pricing power and brand premium.
  - **Medium-term tailwind:** Accelerates industry consolidation. Sungrow, as the global #1 inverter maker, benefits from weaker competitors exiting. The policy is essentially a supply-side reform tool.
  - **Net assessment: Modestly negative near-term, positive medium-term.**

### 3.2 China: New-Type Energy Storage Capacity Pricing Mechanism

- **Landmark Policy:** In January 2026, NDRC and NEA jointly issued the "Notice on Improving Generation-Side Capacity Pricing Mechanisms" (Document No. 114/2026), which **for the first time at the national level** formally established a capacity pricing mechanism for new-type energy storage.
- **Mechanism:** Grid-side independent energy storage stations can receive capacity compensation payments. Provinces can reference coal-fired power capacity prices and adjust based on discharge duration and peak-shaving contribution.
- **Provincial Rollout:** By January 2026, 9 provinces (Hubei, Gansu, etc.) had already issued local storage capacity compensation policies. Industry expects rapid provincial expansion in 2026.
- **Revenue Impact:** Industry estimates suggest a single storage station could earn an additional CNY 10 million+ annually from capacity payments, making standalone storage IRRs viable at 6-12%.
- **Government Work Report:** The 2026 Two Sessions explicitly stated "develop new-type energy storage" as a national priority, with consensus that forced-allocation storage policies will gradually be replaced by market-driven mechanisms.
- **Impact on Sungrow:** This is a **game-changing policy** for Sungrow's storage business. The capacity pricing mechanism transforms energy storage from a policy-mandated cost center into a commercially viable investment proposition. This directly drives demand for Sungrow's utility-scale storage systems. **Strongly positive.**

### 3.3 China: Industry Supply-Side Discipline

## 4. International / Geopolitical Factors

### 4.1 United States: OBBBA Replaces IRA (Signed July 4, 2025)

The **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)** fundamentally reshaped the US clean energy tax credit landscape:

**Key Provisions Affecting Sungrow:**
- **Section 48E (Clean Electricity ITC):** Wind and solar facilities beginning construction after July 4, 2026 are ineligible if placed in service after December 31, 2027. However, **energy storage technology is explicitly exempt from this placed-in-service deadline** — a critical distinction for Sungrow's storage business.
- **Section 45X (Advanced Manufacturing PTC):** Credits for eligible components continue but with enhanced domestic content requirements from January 2027 onward (65% direct material cost from US-manufactured primary components).
- **FEOC Restrictions:** Prohibits projects from receiving "material assistance" from Prohibited Foreign Entities (PFEs), which explicitly includes Chinese entities (government-related, military-listed, or controlled by Chinese nationals). This effectively bars Chinese companies from directly participating in US tax-credit-eligible projects.
- **Residential Solar Credit (Section 25D):** Terminated December 31, 2025.

**Implications for Sungrow:**
- Sungrow cannot directly supply US tax-credit-eligible projects due to FEOC restrictions. The company must rely on third-party integrators or localized manufacturing.
- The exemption of **energy storage from the accelerated solar/wind phase-out** is positive — it means US storage demand continues with ITC support beyond 2027.
- US BESS market is projected at 70 GWh/35 GW in 2026 (SEIA/Benchmark), creating massive demand even with FEOC headwinds.

### 4.2 United States: Section 301 Tariffs on Chinese Solar/Storage Products

- **Solar Cells from China:** 50% Section 301 tariff (effective since 2024), plus 10% Section 122 tariff = **60%+ combined rate.**
- **Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS):** Section 301 tariff increased from 7.5% to **25%** effective January 1, 2026.
- **Inverters:** Currently subject to 25% Section 301 tariff on Chinese-origin products.
- **Net Effect:** Chinese solar products are effectively locked out of direct US sales. Sungrow's US strategy must rely on localized production or third-country supply chains.

### 4.3 European Union: Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA) — Proposed March 4, 2026

The EU proposed the IAA as part of the Clean Industrial Deal, introducing "Made in EU" requirements for publicly funded clean energy projects:

- **Solar PV:** By approximately 2030 (~Year 3 after entry into force), publicly funded projects must use EU-manufactured inverters and solar cells.
- **BESS:** By ~2028 (Year 1), BESS must be assembled in EU; by ~2030 (Year 3), must include EU-manufactured battery cells.
- **Current Status:** This is a **proposal only** — requires EU Parliament and Council approval. Realistic enforcement: 2029-2031 at earliest.
- **Escape Clauses:** Projects can bypass "Made in EU" rules if compliance increases costs by >30% or causes >7-month delay.
- **WTO Risk:** Multiple legal firms flag elevated WTO challenge risk.

**Impact on Sungrow:**
- **Near-term (2026-2028):** Minimal direct impact. The IAA is not yet law and won't be enforced until ~2029+. Private/residential market (majority of Sungrow's European sales) is unaffected.
- **Medium-term (2029+):** If enacted, the utility-scale/public procurement segment (~30-40% of EU solar market) would require EU-origin inverters, creating a structural barrier. Sungrow would need to establish EU manufacturing or JV arrangements.
- **Mitigation:** The EU-India FTA (negotiations concluded January 2026) could create an alternative compliance path. The 30% cost derogation clause may also provide flexibility.
- **Net assessment: Low near-term risk, moderate medium-term strategic concern.**
