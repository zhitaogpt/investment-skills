## 2. Monetary and Fiscal Policy Direction

### PBOC (People's Bank of China) Monetary Policy

- **Policy Stance:** "Moderately accommodative" (适度宽松) -- the strongest easing language in over a decade, confirmed at the March 2026 Two Sessions.
- **LPR Status:** Both 1Y (3.0%) and 5Y (3.5%) LPR have been unchanged for 10 consecutive months as of March 2026.
- **Rate Cut Expectations:** Multiple analysts (Dongfang Jincheng, 21st Century Business Herald) expect the PBOC to cut rates and RRR in 2026, with the timing dependent on the yuan exchange rate and external factors. The government work report shifted language from "push down" financing costs to "maintain at low levels," suggesting a measured pace.
- **Structural Easing:** In January 2026, the PBOC launched a package of structural monetary-financial measures, including targeted re-lending rate cuts to support key sectors.
- **Liquidity Condition:** Ample. The PBOC has maintained supportive liquidity conditions, with experts widely expecting at least one RRR cut in 2026.

**Impact on Innolight:** Neutral-to-slightly-positive. Innolight is primarily an export-oriented company (86.8% overseas revenue in 2024) with strong profitability (net margin ~28%), so domestic monetary policy is not a primary driver. However, accommodative conditions support: (a) capital market valuations, (b) domestic AI infrastructure investment, and (c) expansion financing.

### US Federal Reserve

- **Current Rate:** 3.50-3.75% (held steady at March 18, 2026 FOMC meeting, unanimous vote).
- **Dot Plot:** Median projection still shows 1 rate cut in 2026, but the March minutes (released April 8) revealed "growing openness to rate hikes" among some officials due to war-related oil price shock pushing up inflation.
- **Key Risk:** The March CPI surge to 3.3% may complicate the Fed's path. If inflation remains elevated, the Fed could hold rates for longer or even consider hikes, which would tighten financial conditions.
- **Next Decision:** April 28-29, 2026.

**Impact on Innolight:** Mixed. Tighter US monetary conditions could theoretically slow corporate investment broadly, but hyperscaler AI capex has proven almost entirely decoupled from interest rate cycles. The risk is more about general market sentiment and tech stock valuation multiples if rates stay higher-for-longer.

## 3. Industry-Specific Policy and Regulation Changes

### US-China Trade / Tariff Policy on Optical Modules

This is the **single most critical policy variable** for Innolight.

**Current Tariff Structure (as of April 2026):**

| Export Route | Tariff Rate | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| China direct to US | ~27.5% effective (45% nominal) | 301 tariff of 7.5% + other levies; partially offset by exemptions |
| Thailand to US | **0%** (with tariff exemption) | Optical modules classified under HTS 8517.62.0090; exempt from Section 122 global tariff; only 7.5% 301 tariff applies, further offset by exemptions |
| Optical modules (HTS 8517.62.0090) | Exempt from 10% Section 122 global tariff | White House classified as "certain electronic products" |

**Key Developments:**
- **Innolight's Thailand factory** is now fully operational for complete optical module production and shipping. The company confirmed (March 2026 earnings call) that it can produce all products in Thailand, with chip procurement also routed through Thailand.
- **Export surge:** Jiangsu province data shows Innolight's optical module exports to Thailand, Malaysia, and US totaled RMB 12 billion in Jan-Feb 2026, versus RMB 3.4 billion in Jan-Feb 2025 -- a **3.5x increase YoY**.
- **Tariff exemption status:** Products containing >=20% US-origin technology/components (e.g., Broadcom DSP chips in 800G/1.6T modules) qualify for tariff exemption. Innolight has confirmed its products meet this threshold.
- **Risk factor:** The US-Thailand "reciprocal tariff" agreement sets 19% tariff on Thai goods and 40% on transshipment goods. However, optical modules have been specifically exempted as "certain electronic products." This exemption could be revoked.

### MATCH Act (April 2026)

- **What:** The "Multilateral Assessment of Technology for Hardware Regulation Act" introduced on April 2-3, 2026 by bipartisan US lawmakers. Aims to restrict DUV chipmaking equipment exports to China (going beyond the existing EUV ban).
- **Scope:** Targets semiconductor manufacturing equipment, not optical modules directly. However, it signals an escalation in US tech containment of China.
- **Impact on Innolight:** Indirect. The MATCH Act targets chip fabrication, not optical components. However, if US tech export controls broaden further, there is a tail risk that optical module components or manufacturing equipment could eventually be affected. More immediately, the Act reinforces the "technology sovereignty" trend that drives both US and Chinese hyperscalers to invest in AI infrastructure.

### US AI Chip Export Controls

- **March 2026:** The Trump administration unexpectedly withdrew the Biden-era "AI Diffusion" proposed rule on March 13, 2026, which would have globally restricted AI chip exports. This was interpreted as a relaxation signal.
- **January 2026:** BIS revised review policies, conditionally allowing sales of NVIDIA H200 and AMD equivalents to approved Chinese buyers (with revenue-sharing).
- **Impact on Innolight:** Net positive. If Chinese companies gain some access to advanced AI chips (even restricted versions), they will need optical modules to connect them. Conversely, any severe tightening of AI chip access to China would limit domestic AI build-out, though Innolight's primary revenue comes from overseas hyperscalers, not Chinese customers.

**Bottom Line:** The macro and policy environment for Innolight Technology is among the most favorable of any A-share company. The unprecedented $650-690 billion hyperscaler AI capex cycle in 2026 -- a near-doubling from 2025 -- is the dominant macro factor, and it directly drives demand for Innolight's 800G and 1.6T optical modules. The company's Thailand manufacturing strategy has effectively mitigated the US-China tariff risk (0% effective tariff from Thailand). Chinese industrial policy is explicitly supportive of optical module technology upgrades. The primary macro risks are (a) potential revocation of the US tariff exemption for electronic products, (b) further escalation of US tech export controls, and (c) broader market valuation compression if US inflation persists and the Fed keeps rates elevated. None of these risks currently appear likely to derail the structural growth trajectory.
