## 3. Industry-Specific Policy and Regulation Changes

### US Tariff Impact on Toy/Collectibles Exports

- **Current US Tariff Regime:** US tariffs on Chinese-made toys have undergone dramatic changes. Following the 2025 tariff escalation under IEEPA, tariffs on Chinese toys peaked at 145% before being reduced to approximately 20-30% as of early 2026. The US raised global import tariffs from 10% to 15% in February 2026. Section 301 rates remain as high as 100% on select Chinese imports. [Source: Xinhua English Feb 20, 2026; Yahoo Finance Apr 1, 2026; Trump announcement via xinhuanet.com Feb 22, 2026]

- **Impact on Pop Mart's US Business:** Pop Mart's Labubu line saw US retail prices increase from $22 to $28 (approx. +27%) in response to tariffs in 2025. Pop Mart's COO stated that "tariffs have some impact but not as much as feared, because tariffs are based on production cost, not retail price." The company's high gross margins (collectibles commanding premium pricing) provide a natural buffer. [Source: 36kr/Pop Mart earnings call, October 2025; World Journal, May 2025]

- **Supply Chain Diversification -- Key Mitigation Strategy:** Pop Mart has proactively established six global production bases:
  - **China** (legacy base)
  - **Indonesia** (new, operational since Jan 2026)
  - **Cambodia** (new, operational since Jan 2026)
  - **Mexico** (new, operational since Jan 2026 -- specifically targeting North American market to mitigate US-China tariffs)
  
  The Mexico base is strategically critical: products manufactured in Mexico for the North American market can potentially leverage USMCA trade agreement benefits, significantly reducing or eliminating tariff exposure on US-bound goods. [Source: Caixin, January 8, 2026; Sina Finance, January 6, 2026; Pop Mart official announcement]

### EU Regulatory Changes

- **EU Small Parcel Tax Reform:** From July 1, 2026, the EU will abolish the EUR 150 de minimis exemption for small parcels, imposing a flat EUR 3 customs duty per item. By 2028, full standard customs duties will apply. This primarily targets low-value e-commerce (Shein, Temu) rather than Pop Mart, whose products typically retail above EUR 20-50 per unit. However, it signals a tightening regulatory environment for cross-border consumer goods. [Source: EU Council decision; Sina Finance, February 27, 2026; Epoch Times, March 27, 2026]

- **Impact Assessment for Pop Mart:** Limited direct impact. Pop Mart sells through owned retail stores, pop-up shops, and its own app in Europe -- not primarily through marketplace/direct-mail channels. The EUR 3 per-item surcharge is immaterial for products priced at EUR 20-100+.

### Thailand Import Tariff

- **Thailand New Rule:** From January 1, 2026, Thailand's customs began imposing import duties on all e-commerce goods valued at THB 1 and above, with rates up to 30%. This could modestly impact Pop Mart's online-to-consumer channel in Thailand, though the company's physical store presence mitigates this. [Source: 21jingji.com, January 5, 2026]

## 4. International / Geopolitical Factors

### US-China Trade Relations

- **Current Status:** The trade relationship remains tense but has shown some stabilization. In February 2026, the US terminated the additional ad valorem tariffs under IEEPA (the most extreme tariffs from 2025), but replaced them with a new 15% global tariff baseline plus various sector-specific rates. The net effective tariff on Chinese goods remains elevated but has moderated from 2025 peak levels. [Source: MOFCOM spokesperson, February 24, 2026; cifnews.com, March 2, 2026]

- **Pop Mart-Specific Risk:** Pop Mart's US revenue exposure is still relatively small compared to Southeast Asia. Morgan Stanley (April 2025) maintained a positive long-term view on Pop Mart's global potential despite tariff headwinds. The company's Mexico production base is a strategic hedge against further US-China tariff escalation. [Source: Morgan Stanley research, April 7, 2025; baijing.cn]

### Middle East Geopolitical Risk

- **G7 Emergency Meeting:** The G7 energy, finance, and central bank chiefs held emergency meetings to assess the impact of Middle East conflicts on energy, economy, and financial stability. This introduces uncertainty into global consumer confidence and could affect discretionary spending patterns. [Source: cnfin.com, April 7, 2026]

### Currency / Exchange Rate Dynamics

- **USD/CNY:** The RMB has appreciated meaningfully in 2026, from approximately 6.99 at the start of the year to 6.82-6.83 as of mid-April 2026 -- a roughly 2.4% appreciation. The PBOC set the mid-rate at 6.868 on April 8, 2026. [Source: PBOC via Sina Finance, April 8, 2026; exchange-rates.org]

- **HKD/CNY:** As of April 15, 2026, 1 HKD = approximately 0.8695 CNY (or 1 CNY = 1.149 HKD). The HKD/CNY rate has been relatively stable given the HKD's peg to the USD. [Source: Yahoo Finance HKD/CNY history]

- **Implications for Pop Mart:**
  - **Positive:** RMB appreciation against USD reduces the RMB-denominated cost of imported raw materials and components.
  - **Negative:** RMB strength makes Chinese-manufactured exports less competitive on price, though Pop Mart's pricing power (IP-driven, collectible premiums) largely insulates it from this effect.
  - **Translation Risk:** As overseas revenues (denominated in USD, EUR, THB, JPY, KRW, etc.) are translated back to RMB for financial reporting, a stronger RMB compresses reported overseas revenue growth. Given overseas revenue now represents a substantial and growing share of total revenue, this translation headwind is non-trivial.
  - **HKD-listed Stock:** Pop Mart's stock price is quoted in HKD, which is pegged to the USD. A weakening USD/HKD versus CNY means mainland investors via Southbound Stock Connect effectively pay more RMB per HKD share.
