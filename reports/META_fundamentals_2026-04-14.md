## 1. Company Overview and Business Model

Meta Platforms, Inc. is a global technology conglomerate headquartered in Menlo Park, California, operating the world's largest social media ecosystem. The company operates through two reportable segments:

**Family of Apps (FoA)** -- the dominant revenue engine -- encompasses Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, Threads, and Meta AI. This segment generated $198.8 billion in FY2025 revenue (98.9% of total) and $102.5 billion in operating income (operating margin: 51.6%). The core monetization model is digital advertising, with $196.2 billion in ad revenue (97.6% of total revenue) driven by 3.58 billion daily active people (DAP), up 7% YoY.

**Reality Labs (RL)** includes VR/AR consumer hardware (Meta Quest), AI glasses (Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta), the Meta Neural Band wrist device, and the Meta Ray-Ban Display. This segment generated $2.2 billion in FY2025 revenue but posted a $19.2 billion operating loss, reflecting Meta's long-term bet on immersive computing and spatial platforms.

**Key strategic pillars for 2026:**
- **AI Monetization:** Meta AI assistant rollout across all apps; AI-powered ad recommendation systems drove 18% impression growth and 6% price-per-ad growth in Q4 2025.
- **Superintelligence Infrastructure:** Meta Superintelligence Labs is driving a massive $115-135 billion CapEx plan for 2026, nearly doubling 2025's $72.2 billion.
- **Reels and Short-Form Video:** Instagram original content now comprises 75% of US recommendations; Threads time spent increased 20% in Q4.
- **Business Messaging / Commerce:** WhatsApp business tools and click-to-message ads remain under-monetized growth vectors.

The company employs 78,865 people (up 6% YoY) and is led by founder/CEO Mark Zuckerberg, who retains voting control.

### Peer Comparison Takeaways

5. **Growth trade-off:** META's revenue growth (22%) outpaces GOOGL (18%) and AMZN (14%), justifying its P/S premium over AMZN but not explaining its discount to GOOGL.

## 4. Valuation Analysis

### 4.1 P/S Ratio

**P/S = Market Cap / Annual Revenue = $1,605B / $201.0B = 7.99x**

This is below GOOGL (9.6x) and near the sector median for large-cap tech. Given META's 82% gross margin and 22% revenue growth, the P/S appears reasonable.

### 4.2 PEG Ratio

Using TTM earnings growth of 10.7% (reported):

**PEG = Trailing P/E / Earnings Growth = 27.0 / 10.7 = 2.52x**

However, this is distorted by the one-time tax charge. Using the adjusted earnings growth (excluding the OBBBA tax impact, FY2025 adjusted net income ~$75B vs FY2024 $62.4B = ~20% growth):

**Adjusted PEG = 27.0 / 20.0 = 1.35x**

Using forward consensus EPS growth of ~15.6% (per Simply Wall St):

**Forward PEG = 27.0 / 15.6 = 1.73x**

A PEG between 1.0-1.5 is generally considered fairly valued; 1.35-1.73x suggests META is reasonably priced to slightly expensive on a growth-adjusted basis, depending on which growth rate is used.

### 4.3 Earnings Yield

**Earnings Yield = EPS / Price = $23.49 / $634.53 = 3.70%**

This compares to the 10-year US Treasury yield of approximately 4.3-4.5%. At 3.70%, META's earnings yield is slightly below the risk-free rate, which is typical for growth companies where earnings are expected to grow meaningfully. The forward earnings yield (using FY2026E EPS of ~$35.7, based on forward P/E of 17.8) would be approximately 5.63%, which is more attractive.

### 4.4 FCF Yield

**FCF Yield = Operating Cash Flow / Market Cap = $115.8B / $1,605B = 7.21%** (operating CF basis)

**FCF Yield = Free Cash Flow / Market Cap = $43.6B / $1,605B = 2.72%** (after CapEx)

The 7.2% OCF yield is excellent, reflecting the immense cash generation power of the advertising business. The 2.7% FCF yield reflects the CapEx drag. With 2026 CapEx guided at $115-135B versus projected OCF of ~$130-140B, the FCF yield in 2026 could compress to approximately 0.3-1.6%. This is the key investor concern.

- Revenue Growth: Year 1: 18% (guided mid-20s growth minus conservatism for macro), Year 2: 16%, Year 3: 14%, Year 4: 12%, Year 5: 10%

- Tax Rate: 14% (normalized, per management guidance of 13-16%)

- CapEx as % of Revenue: Year 1: 50%, Year 2: 42%, Year 3: 35%, Year 4: 30%, Year 5: 28%
- Discount Rate (WACC): 10%
- Terminal Growth Rate: 3%

- **Sum of PV of FCFs:** $99.9 billion
- **Terminal Value:** FCF_Y5 x (1 + g) / (WACC - g) = $68.2B x 1.03 / 0.07 = $1,003.6 billion
- **PV of Terminal Value:** $1,003.6B x 0.621 = $623.3 billion

**Important DCF caveat:** This base-case DCF yields a value ($285) well below the current price, driven almost entirely by the assumption that 2026 CapEx will consume virtually all operating cash flow. This model is highly sensitive to (a) how quickly CapEx normalizes, and (b) whether AI infrastructure spending generates commensurate revenue growth.

**Optimistic DCF Scenario** (CapEx normalizes faster, AI drives higher growth):
- Revenue growth: 22%, 20%, 18%, 15%, 12%
- CapEx as % of Revenue: 48%, 38%, 30%, 25%, 22%
- Operating Margin: 40% sustained
- This yields an equity value of approximately $1.55 trillion, or **~$613 per share**

**Bull-case DCF** (AI-driven reacceleration + operating leverage):
- Revenue growth: 25%, 22%, 20%, 18%, 15%
- CapEx as % of Revenue: 46%, 35%, 28%, 24%, 22%
- Operating Margin: expanding from 40% to 44% as AI drives efficiency
- This yields an equity value of approximately $2.15 trillion, or **~$850 per share**

## 5. Risk Factors

**Upside Risks:**
- AI-driven advertising efficiency could accelerate revenue growth beyond consensus
- Meta AI and business messaging monetization represents a large TAM
- CapEx cycle may peak sooner if Meta achieves infrastructure efficiencies
- Share buybacks at current valuation levels are highly accretive

**Downside Risks:**
- $115-135B CapEx in 2026 could depress FCF to near-zero levels
- Reality Labs losses show no path to breakeven in the medium term
- EU regulatory headwinds (Less Personalized Ads) could meaningfully impact European revenue (~25% of total)
- US youth safety litigation could result in "material loss" per management's own disclosure
- JPMorgan's massive 59.9% position reduction in Q4 2025 is a notable institutional exit
- Insider selling is routine (10b5-1 plans) but CFO Li's $84M in sales is on the larger side

## 8. Conclusion

Meta Platforms is a fundamentally exceptional business trading at a reasonable valuation. The Family of Apps segment is one of the most profitable businesses in the world, with a 51.6% operating margin serving 3.58 billion daily users. Revenue growth of 22% at this scale is remarkable and driven by structural tailwinds in AI-optimized ad targeting.

The primary concern is the unprecedented $115-135 billion CapEx plan for 2026, which will likely consume most or all of the company's operating cash flow. This represents a calculated bet by Zuckerberg on AI infrastructure -- specifically Meta Superintelligence Labs -- that will either prove visionary (as the 2023-2024 efficiency investments did) or become a significant drag on shareholder returns.

At $634.53, the stock trades near the lower end of our $650-$850 fair value range, offering meaningful upside (18-34%) to the midpoint and consensus target ($856), while providing a reasonable margin of safety from the core ad business's cash generation power. The 17.8x forward P/E represents a 25-30% discount to mega-cap peers, which appears too generous a discount for a company with superior growth and margins.

**Rating: 7.6/10 -- Fundamentally strong with near-term CapEx overhang.**
